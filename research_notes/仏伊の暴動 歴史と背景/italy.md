# Italy: Protests, Riots and Civil Unrest (current to 19 Oct 2026, with historical background)

Method note: researched 2026-10-19 via web search in Italian and English. WebFetch failed with DNS errors (aljazeera.com, france24.com, democrata.es), so most findings come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not full-text reads. Casualty and arrest figures for the 2026 events were provisional and varied between outlets. Discrepancies are flagged inline. Labels: [CURRENT] = Sept 2025 to Oct 2026; [HISTORICAL] = earlier.

## 1. What unrest occurred in late 2025 and in 2026 up to October? Triggers, scale, arrests, injuries

### Takeaway
Italy has had about 13 months of rolling unrest, in four phases:
1. Pro-Gaza and Flotilla general strikes (Sept to Nov 2025), the largest mobilisations in years.
2. A cycle of violence centred on the "antagonist" milieu, tied to the eviction of Turin's Askatasuna social centre (Dec 2025 to Jan 2026) and the Milan-Cortina Olympics (Feb 2026).
3. Anti-war, anti-rearmament and far-right "remigration" street politics (May to June 2026).
4. An energy-driven cost-of-living revolt in October 2026. Its 17 Oct riots in Milan, Turin, Bologna, Brescia and Bergamo left 141 police injured and 28 arrested (Viminale provisional count). Many of those detained were minors.

**A. Pro-Gaza / Global Sumud Flotilla strikes, Sept to Nov 2025 [CURRENT, late 2025]**
- 22 Sept 2025: base unions (USB and others) called a general strike for Gaza that brought protests to many Italian cities. Organisers estimated "hundreds of thousands" took part. The worst clashes were in Milan, around Milano Centrale station. — [Euronews 23 Sep 2025](https://www.euronews.com/2025/09/23/general-strike-brings-protests-across-italian-cities-over-gaza-war); [Wikipedia: 2025 Italian general strikes and protests for Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_general_strikes_and_protests_for_Gaza)
- 22 Sept 2025, Milan: about a hundred young people tried to force their way into Milano Centrale, throwing smoke bombs, and police responded with batons. Il Sole 24 Ore reported more than 10 people detained and 60 officers injured in Milan. Meloni called the scenes "unworthy images". — [Il Sole 24 Ore (EN)](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/today-general-strike-gaza-l-impact-services-demonstration-rome-AHmn85kC)
- Totals for the whole wave (19 Sept to 29 Nov 2025), per Wikipedia: at least 187 injured (including 60 in Milan) and at least 49 arrested across Milan, Bologna and Udine. This is an aggregate from a secondary source, not an official figure. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_general_strikes_and_protests_for_Gaza)
- 3 Oct 2025: CGIL and the base unions held a national general strike after Israel intercepted the Global Sumud Flotilla, which carried Italian participants. Hundreds of trains were cancelled or delayed, some domestic flights were cancelled and many schools closed. — [Euronews 3 Oct 2025](https://www.euronews.com/2025/10/03/italy-paralysed-by-nationwide-strike-in-solidarity-with-intercepted-gaza-aid-flotilla); [Malay Mail 3 Oct 2025](https://www.malaymail.com/news/world/2025/10/03/train-chaos-in-italy-as-unions-strike-in-support-of-gaza-aid-flotilla/193302)

  - Milan: CGIL said about 100,000 marched; local police said 50,000 (as reported by Rai). — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/10/03/italy-paralysed-by-nationwide-strike-in-solidarity-with-intercepted-gaza-aid-flotilla); [Yahoo/Reuters syndication](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/italy-paralysed-nationwide-strike-solidarity-161042784.html)
  - Nationally: the unions claimed more than 2 million strikers or participants. This is an organiser claim, with no independent verification found. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Italian_general_strikes_and_protests_for_Gaza)
  - Rome: one summary cited a Questura estimate of about 30,000 demonstrators. The exact date and attribution could not be confirmed with a full-text read.

**B. 28 Nov 2025 base-union general strike and the attack on La Stampa (Turin) [CURRENT]**
- 28 Nov 2025: Cobas, USB, SGB and CUB held a 24-hour national general strike covering transport, schools and hospitals. Journalists struck separately the same day: FNSI called the strike over a national contract that had been expired for 10 years. — [Euronews IT 28 Nov 2025](https://it.euronews.com/2025/11/28/sciopero-generale-in-tutta-italia-a-rischio-treni-bus-e-aerei-protestano-anche-i-giornalis); [Today.it](https://www.today.it/attualita/sciopero-generale-oggi-28-novembre-2025.html)

  - The action was linked to the case of Mohamed Shahin, an imam facing expulsion.
  - Condemnation was bipartisan: regional president Cirio, mayor Lo Russo, FNSI and the Ordine dei Giornalisti all spoke against it. It was widely called a "squadrista" assault.
  - Sources: [Virgilio](https://www.virgilio.it/notizie/sciopero-generale-28-novembre-assalto-alla-redazione-de-la-stampa-lancio-di-letame-sfondata-porta-di-vetro-1719059); [TorinoToday](https://www.torinotoday.it/cronaca/irruzione-manifestanti-sciopero-generale-la-stampa-mohamed-shahin-letame-cancelli-28-novembre-2025.html); [Gazzetta di Parma 29 Nov 2025](https://www.gazzettadiparma.it/italia-mondo/2025/11/29/news/assaltata-la-sede-della-stampa-da-un-centinaio-di-manifestanti-908868/); [Unione Monregalese](https://unionemonregalese.it/news/la-cronaca/305581/assalto-alla-redazione-de-la-stampa-durante-lo-sciopero-generale-a-torino.html)

**C. Askatasuna eviction and Turin riot, 31 Jan 2026 [CURRENT]**

- 31 Jan 2026: a large national march in support of Askatasuna was peaceful for most of its length, according to Il Post. At the end, part of the march left the agreed route and headed for the evicted building.
  - Protesters threw devices at police, police fired tear gas, and a police armoured vehicle was set on fire. A Rai TV crew was attacked.

**G. "Autumn of discontent": cost-of-living / energy protests, Oct 2026 [CURRENT, most recent]**

### Inferences
- The pattern repeats across 2025 and 2026: a large, mostly peaceful march (union, student or pro-Palestine), then a breakaway of a hundred or so masked youths from the antagonist or anarchist milieu who clash with police. The government then uses the breakaway violence to frame the whole movement.
- The October 2026 wave differs from the 2025 Gaza wave in three ways. It is driven by material grievances (energy inflation at 4.2%), not foreign policy. It spans social groups, from students to truckers and farmers. And minors make up a very large share of those detained. Taken together, these point to a broader and younger base of discontent heading into the 2027 general election.
- Turin keeps reappearing as the epicentre: Askatasuna, La Stampa, "No Meloni Day", the 10 minors with Molotovs. That links the current events to the No TAV and antagonist ecosystem of the Susa valley and Turin.

## 2. Who are the actors and what are their aims?

### Takeaway
Three distinct camps.
- Mass, legal mobilisers: CGIL, the base unions (USB, Cobas, CUB, SGB), student collectives and pro-Palestine coalitions. Their aims are wages, the cost of living, Gaza, anti-rearmament and education.
- A small, organised antagonist and anarchist milieu around social centres such as Askatasuna. It does much of the street fighting and sabotage.
- A far-right street sector (CasaPound, Forza Nuova and allied groups) that now mobilises around "remigration".

Party politics maps onto this split. FdI, Lega and FI frame unrest as public-order crime and press the left to condemn it. PD, M5S and AVS condemn the violence but defend the grievances. The two coalitions are now neck and neck in the polls.

## 3. How does the Meloni government frame and respond? Security decrees and their critics

### Takeaway
The government has passed two successive security packages.
- DL 48/2025, converted into Law 80 of 9 Jun 2025: new crimes and tougher penalties, including criminalisation of road and rail blockades, passive resistance and occupations.
- DL 23/2026 of 24 Feb 2026, converted in April 2026, passed weeks after the Turin riot: new police powers, including up to 12 hours' preventive detention of people deemed a risk to the peaceful conduct of demonstrations.

Critics have called the 2025 package "the biggest attack on the right to protest in the history of the Republic". They include Antigone, CILD, CIVICUS, the bar associations (UCPI), the magistrates' association (ANM), the Cassazione's research office, and five UN special rapporteurs.

### Cited Findings
- DL 11 Apr 2025 no. 48:
  - In force from 12 Apr 2025.
  - The Chamber approved conversion on 29 May 2025 by 163 to 91 (1 abstention), after a confidence vote.
  - The decree was largely the earlier "DDL sicurezza" bill re-issued as a decree, with some Quirinale (presidency) constitutional objections taken on board.
  - Converted into Law no. 80 of 9 Jun 2025.
  - Sources: [Internazionale 8 Apr 2025](https://www.internazionale.it/notizie/annalisa-camilli/2025/04/08/cosa-prevede-il-decreto-sicurezza); [Edotto (Cassazione analysis)](https://www.edotto.com/articolo/decreto-sicurezza-2025-analisi-della-corte-di-cassazione); [Sadefenza](https://sadefenza.substack.com/p/conferenza-sul-decreto-sicurezza)
- Content (per Domani and Internazionale): new crimes and tougher penalties around demonstrations, blockades and passive resistance; the crime of "arbitrary occupation" of buildings; and a new crime of prison riot that covers passive resistance. — [Domani](https://www.editorialedomani.it/fatti/decreto-sicurezza-nuovi-reati-pene-inasprite-manifestazioni-proteste-resistenza-passiva-cosa-cambia-hrglls7g); [Rapporto Antigone](https://www.rapportoantigone.it/ventunesimo-rapporto-sulle-condizioni-di-detenzione/il-decreto-sicurezza-e-il-delitto-illegittimo-di-rivolta-penitenziaria/)

  - Sources: [L'Unità 5 Apr 2025](https://www.unita.it/2025/04/05/decreto-sicurezza-latto-di-accusa-di-antigone-modifiche-irrilevanti-e-un-provvedimento-illiberale/); [Antigone](https://www.antigone.it/news/3559-ddl-sicurezza-antigone-il-piu-grande-attacco-alla-liberta-di-protesta-della-storia-repubblicana-il-senato-lo-fermi); [CIVICUS Lens](https://lens.civicus.org/interview/il-decreto-sicurezza-e-il-piu-grande-attacco-al-diritto-di-protesta-della-storia-della-repubblica-italiana/)

  - The economic response to the Oct 2026 unrest was fuel-tax relief and a petrol price cap below EUR 2 per litre agreed with distributors.

### Inferences
- The timeline suggests DL 23/2026 was at least partly a response to the 31 Jan 2026 Turin riot and the Olympics-period sabotage: it was issued about 3 weeks after Turin. Its preventive 12-hour detention power will likely be tested in the Oct to Nov 2026 protest cycle.

## 4. Socioeconomic data (wages, youth unemployment, emigration, poverty)

### Takeaway
Italy's distinctive structural grievance is real wages that in 2025 were still below their 1990 level (OECD). On top of that sit:
- 20.3% youth unemployment (Aug 2026).
- 5.4 million people (9.3%) in absolute poverty in 2025, concentrated in the South, among renters and among foreign households.
- Sustained emigration of young graduates.
- A new energy-inflation shock (4.2% in Sept 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Real wages: according to the OECD Economic Survey of Italy 2026 (Apr 2026), average real wages in 2025 were still below their 1990 level, reflecting stagnant productivity and inflation.
  - Wanted in Rome, citing an OECD-based report, describes Italy as the only EU country where real wages fell after 1990. An economist (Heimberger) estimated real wages in 2023 were 4.4% below 1990; this comes from social media.
  - An AFP-based summary gives a fall of 6.6% between 1990 and 2026. This is less verifiable.
  - Sources: [OECD Economic Surveys: Italy 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/04/oecd-economic-surveys-italy-2026_3fd3b6aa/full-report/engaging-youth-in-the-labour-market-amid-population-ageing_9ff77198.html); [Wanted in Rome](https://www.wantedinrome.com/news/italy-has-the-lowest-wages-among-major-european-countries.html); [Heimberger on X](https://x.com/heimbergecon/status/1808437500114858441)
- Inflation: ISTAT put September 2026 inflation at 4.2%, driven by fuel prices. — [The Local 16 Oct 2026](https://www.thelocal.it/20261016/italy-braces-for-autumn-of-discontent-as-students-protest)
- Unemployment (ISTAT, Aug 2026, provisional, released 1 Oct 2026):
  - Overall: 6.2% (+0.2 points).
  - Youth (15–24): 20.3% (+0.6 points).
  - Ages 25–34: 9.7%.
  - Job seekers rose by 48,000 in a month.
  - Employment was +291,000 year on year (+1.2%), but fell among 15–24-year-olds.
  - Inactivity (15–64) was 32.7%, the lowest since 2004.
  - Sources: [Il Fatto Quotidiano 1 Oct 2026](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2026/10/01/istat-agosto-occupati-tasso-disoccupazione-giovanile-confcommercio-occupazione-notizie/8524200/); [CanaleUno](https://www.canaleuno.it/2026/10/01/lavoro-istat-ad-agosto-disoccupazione-sale-al-62-per-giovani-al-203/); [Corriere Università](https://corriereuniv.it/disoccupazione-giovanile-agosto-2026/); [Dottrinalavoro](https://www.dottrinalavoro.it/notizie-c/istat-occupati-e-disoccupati-agosto-2026)

  - People: more than 5.4 million in absolute poverty, 9.3% of residents (9.8% in 2024 per one summary; ISTAT calls it "stable"). Households: 8.0%.
  - By age: 12.5% of minors and 12.1% of 18–34-year-olds (about 1.198 million).
  - By job status: 14.3% of manual workers (15.6% in 2024) and 20.7% of job seekers.
  - By nationality: 5.9% of all-Italian households, 26.7% of households with at least one foreign member, 29.4% of all-foreign households.
  - By housing: 20.9% of renting households (about 999,000) versus 4.5% of owner households.
  - By region: the South is the worst-hit area.
  - Sources: [ISTAT PDF](https://www.istat.it/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/La-poverta-in-Italia-2025.pdf); [Askanews 15 Oct 2026](https://askanews.it/2026/10/15/istat-in-italia-54-milioni-di-persone-in-poverta-assoluta-sud-piu-colpito/); [AGI 15 Oct 2026](https://www.agi.it/economia/news/2026-10-15/istat-poverta-assoluta-39541779/); [Il Fatto Quotidiano 15 Oct 2026](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2026/10/15/poverta-assoluta-istat-2025-notizie/8539130/)
- Emigration (OECD 2026):
  - About 6% of 18–34-year-olds left Italy between 2011 and 2024; elsewhere the same survey says more than 400,000 young people left between 2012 and 2023.
  - Half of young emigrants now hold a degree, up from a third a decade earlier.
  - A Medium post citing ISTAT gives more than 1.3 million citizens emigrating between 2012 and 2022, over 60% of them under 35. This is a secondary source.
  - Sources: [OECD 2026](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/04/oecd-economic-surveys-italy-2026_3fd3b6aa/full-report/engaging-youth-in-the-labour-market-amid-population-ageing_9ff77198.html); [Medium](https://medium.com/@fiorellanatanne/italys-brain-drain-e983540c2907)

### Inferences
- An energy-price shock on top of 30 years of flat real wages, plus a youth unemployment rate rising again, is a plausible material driver of the young, cross-class October 2026 protests.
- High poverty among renters (20.9%) supports the housing and cost-of-living framing.
- High poverty among foreign households (26.7–29.4%) intersects with the far right's "remigration" agenda.
- The north-south divide shows up in the data (the South is worst on poverty). Yet the riots are concentrated in northern cities (Milan, Turin, Bologna, Brescia, Bergamo), where the social-centre and student infrastructure is densest.

## 6. Historical background [HISTORICAL]

### Takeaway
Today's repertoire draws on several historical layers:
- Social centres, autonomia and 1977-style antagonism.
- The memory of the Years of Lead (about 350 killed in 1969–82).
- The Genoa G8 trauma of 2001, which the ECtHR ruled was torture.
- The No TAV movement in the Susa valley, the template for Turin's antagonist scene.
- Recurring neo-fascist street violence, which peaked in the 2021 CGIL HQ assault.

### Cited Findings
- Years of Lead: conventionally dated from the late 1960s to 1988.
  - Avvenire: about 350 dead and 1,100 wounded from terrorism in 1969–1982.
  - Piazza Fontana (12 Dec 1969), usually treated as the start, killed 17 and wounded 88.
  - Sources: [Avvenire](https://www.avvenire.it/attualita/anni-di-piombo-poca-memoria-e-tante-zone-grigie-perche-la-violenza-politica-non-e-sconfitta_108163); [Wikipedia: Years of Lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_(Italy)); [Eroica Fenice](https://www.eroicafenice.com/notizie-attualita/la-strage-di-piazza-fontana-le-vittime-hanno-avuto-giustizia/)
- 1968 student movement and the "Hot Autumn" of 1969 are the precedent invoked by the "autumn of discontent" framing. The 2013 truckers' and farmers' "Forconi" protests are a precedent for the 2026 road blockades. — [Wikipedia: 1968 movement in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_movement_in_Italy); [Wikipedia: 2013 Italian social protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Italian_social_protests)
- Genoa G8, July 2001:
  - In the Diaz school raid, police injured dozens: 82 wounded by one count, 61 seriously, 3 critically. British journalist Mark Covell was left in a coma.
  - The force involved was 346 police plus 149 Carabinieri, per the Genoa Court of Appeal.
  - Sources: [Wikipedia: 2001 raid on Armando Diaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_raid_on_Armando_Diaz); [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/world/europe/italy-found-guilty-of-using-torture-during-g8-protest-1.2167973)
- ECtHR, Cestaro v. Italy (no. 6884/11, 7 Apr 2015): Italy violated Article 3 (torture). The court found Italian anti-torture law inadequate and noted that no officer served jail time because of statutes of limitation. A further ECtHR ruling on 22 Jun 2017 found torture in the raid and the subsequent detention (Bolzaneto). — [Università di Padova Human Rights Centre](https://unipd-centrodirittiumani.it/en/news/european-court-of-human-rights-italy-condemned-for-torture-for-the-facts-occurred-during-the-g8-summit-in-genoa-in-2001); [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/eu-court-blasts-torture-by-italian-police-in-2001-g8-raid/); [Canestrini Lex](https://canestrinilex.com/en/readings/italys-police-forces-tortured)
- 2021 CGIL HQ assault: Forza Nuova-led anti-Green Pass protesters stormed CGIL's Rome headquarters on 9 Oct 2021. — [Euronews 2021](https://www.euronews.com/2021/10/12/us-health-coronavirus-italy-draghi-cgil)
- Youth unemployment as a long-run structural feature. — [Wikipedia: Youth unemployment in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_unemployment_in_Italy)

### Inferences
- The Diaz and Bolzaneto rulings, and Italy's late adoption of a crime of torture (2017), form the core of civil-liberties critics' distrust of expanded police powers under DL 48/2025 and DL 23/2026.
- Government rhetoric that antagonists are "a danger to democracy" draws, implicitly, on the memory of the Years of Lead. Critics instead draw on Genoa 2001.

  - the No TAV movement: its history from 2005 (Venaus), its 2011–2012 clashes at Chiomonte, the "associazione sovversiva" trials, or links to Askatasuna.
  - Carlo Giuliani's death (20 Jul 2001).
  - the 1977 movement.
  - migration statistics (landings, the Albania centres).
  - the North-South GDP gap.
