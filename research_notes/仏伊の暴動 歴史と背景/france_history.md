## Q1. 2005 and 2023 riots: damage, arrests, ages, duration

### Takeaway
The 2023 riots after Nahel Merzouk's death (27 June 2023) were shorter (about one week) but more intense per night than 2005 (27 Oct to about 20 Nov 2005, roughly 3.5 weeks). 2023 saw more attacks on public buildings (2,508 buildings, including 105 town halls and 243 schools) and comparable vehicle burnings (about 12,000 vs about 10,300). Arrests were somewhat lower (about 3,500 vs about 4,700). The average age was about 17-18. The total cost was about EUR 1 billion, of which EUR 650-793 million was insured.

### Cited Findings
**2005 (Clichy-sous-Bois, after Zyed Benna and Bouna Traoré were electrocuted in an EDF substation on 27 Oct 2005)**
- Trigger: Zyed Benna and Bouna Traoré were electrocuted on 27 Oct 2005 in an EDF transformer in Clichy-sous-Bois. The violence lasted about three weeks across about 200 towns, and a state of emergency was declared on 8 Nov 2005 — [Global Media Journal, Garcia & Retis](https://epe.lac-bac.gc.ca/100/201/300/global_media_journal/v02n01/1101/v4i1_garcia%20et%20retis.pdf); [Vikidia](https://fr.vikidia.org/wiki/%C3%89meutes_de_2005_dans_les_banlieues_fran%C3%A7aises)
- Mar 2006 tally by the Interior Ministry, reported by Maire-info: 10,300 vehicles burned (4,200 in Île-de-France), 7 bus depots and 22 buses or train sets, more than 200 public buildings and 74 private buildings destroyed (27 Oct to 20 Nov) — [Maire-info, 29 Mar 2006](https://www.maire-info.com/violences-dans-les-banlieues-un-bilan-quatre-mois-apres-article2-6828). Le Temps instead gives 233 public buildings damaged and more than 6,000 people arrested — [Le Temps](https://www.letemps.ch/monde/france-tire-un-trait-emeutes-2005)
- Arrests: 4,728 during the 3.5 weeks plus 1,328 afterwards (Interior Ministry figures cited by La Gazette) — [La Gazette France](https://www.lagazettefrance.fr/article/des-violences-urbaines-plus-intenses-en-2023-qu-en-2005). Other sources: about 4,700 ([La Vie des idées comparison table, Apr 2024](https://laviedesidees.fr/IMG/pdf/20240412_emeutes.pdf)) and "about 3,000" ([vl-media, weak source](https://vl-media.fr/?p=408415)). There were 5,643 gardes à vue in 2005 ([actu-juridique](https://www.actu-juridique.fr/penal/emeutes-de-juillet-la-mobilisation-massive-des-forces-de-lordre-a-porte-ses-fruits/)).
- A "final national police tally" of 1,295 vehicles, cited by aujourd'hui.ma, conflicts with the roughly 10,000 total. It probably refers to a single night or a partial period — [aujourdhui.ma](https://aujourdhui.ma/?p=45596). The peak night saw 1,408 vehicles burned — [DH.be, 8 Nov 2005](https://www.dhnet.be/actu/monde/2005/11/08/nos-envoyes-speciaux-72SMM5KZJRHUTF73Q4PJN5K234/)
- Deaths: actu-juridique counts five deaths in 2005, mostly linked to arson, including a disabled woman burned in Sevran. Some researchers count only the Sevran case as directly riot-related — [actu-juridique](https://www.actu-juridique.fr/penal/emeutes-de-juillet-la-mobilisation-massive-des-forces-de-lordre-a-porte-ses-fruits/)

**2023 (Nanterre, Nahel Merzouk, 17, shot by a police officer at a traffic stop on 27 June 2023)**
- Senate law commission report (Apr 2024): about 3,500 people arrested, average age 18, about 500 minors, 193 of them aged 15 or under — [Maire-info summary](https://www.maire-info.com/violences-urbaines/neuf-mois-apres-les-emeutes-un-bilan-25-propositions-article-28511)
- Damage (Senate report): 2,508 buildings burned or damaged, including 105 town halls and 243 schools; 12,031 vehicles burned; 1,000 to 1,500 shops vandalised or looted; 2 deaths (Marseille, Cayenne); at least 1,000 injured, including 782 police and gendarmes — [Public Sénat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/parlementaire/emeutes-de-2023-un-cout-colossal-estime-a-un-milliard-deuros-selon-le-rapport-du-senat) and [Maire-info](https://www.maire-info.com/violences-urbaines/neuf-mois-apres-les-emeutes-un-bilan-25-propositions-article-28511)
- Cost: 16,400 insurance claims worth EUR 793 million. The Senate puts the total at about EUR 1 billion because some losses were never declared — [Public Sénat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/parlementaire/emeutes-de-2023-un-cout-colossal-estime-a-un-milliard-deuros-selon-le-rapport-du-senat). France Assureurs first estimated EUR 650 million ([The Local, 11 Jul 2023](https://www.thelocal.fr/20230711/cost-of-french-riots-reaches-e650-million-say-insureres)) and raised it to EUR 730 million on 6 Sep 2023. The government and MEDEF gave an early, unspecified estimate of EUR 1 billion — [economiematin](https://www.economiematin.fr/?p=120431)
- Arrest count over time: about 3,200 detained over five nights, about two-thirds unknown to police (Darmanin) — [thesun.my/AFP](https://thesun.my/?p=340257). At a Senate hearing: 3,505 arrests since 27 June, the youngest aged 11 and the oldest 59 — [Europe 1](https://www.europe1.fr/node/60114). La Vie des idées table: 3,550 (2023) vs 4,700 (2005) — [La Vie des idées PDF](https://laviedesidees.fr/IMG/pdf/20240412_emeutes.pdf)
- No state of emergency was declared in 2023, unlike 2005 — [actu-juridique](https://www.actu-juridique.fr/penal/emeutes-de-juillet-la-mobilisation-massive-des-forces-de-lordre-a-porte-ses-fruits/)
- The Senate report recommends allowing certain social-network features to be blocked during periods of severe unrest (Snapchat and TikTok are seen as accelerants) — [Public Sénat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/parlementaire/emeutes-de-2023-un-rapport-du-senat-preconise-le-blocage-de-certaines-fonctionnalites-des-reseaux-sociaux-en-cas-de-grande-tension)
- Caveat: Maire-info points out an implausible statement in the Senate report's summary ("L'essentiel"), which says nearly three-quarters of minors brought before a judge were enrolled in higher education. It is probably an error — [Maire-info](https://www.maire-info.com/violences-urbaines/neuf-mois-apres-les-emeutes-un-bilan-25-propositions-article-28511)
- An Ifop "Focus 236" note gives a first diagnosis of the 2023 riots, describing more intense violence than 2005 — [Ifop PDF](https://www.ifop.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Focus-236-Emeutes-premiers-elements-de-diagnostic.pdf)

### Inferences
- 2023 was more concentrated than 2005: a vehicle count comparable to 2005 and many more attacks on public buildings, packed into about one week instead of three weeks. That points to a faster spread, plausibly helped by social networks. Symbols of the state (town halls, schools, police stations) were the main targets.
- The "average age 17" figure often quoted for 2023 corresponds to Darmanin's early statements. The Senate final report says 18. The report writer should cite "17-18".

### Gaps
- No source fetched gave the Ministry of Justice's final count of convictions for 2023 (press reports of about 1,300 immediate trials were not verified) or for 2005.
- I could not verify the number of communes affected in 2023 (often quoted as about 500-550).
- I found no directly fetched primary Interior Ministry document for 2005.

## Q2. Socioeconomic data on QPV vs national

### Takeaway
QPV residents are about three times as likely to be poor as the national population (45% vs 15%), have more than twice the unemployment rate (18% vs 7.5%), and youth unemployment of about 30%. Early school leaving is about twice the urban average.

### Cited Findings
- Poverty rate: 45.2% in QPV vs 15.3% for France as a whole (INSEE 2021 data) — [Observatoire des inégalités](https://inegalites.fr/Les-revenus-et-la-pauvrete-dans-les-quartiers-les-plus-en-difficulte)
- Unemployment among 15-64 year-olds in 2022: 18.3% in QPV vs 7.5% in the surrounding urban units (ONPV 2023 report, from INSEE's Enquête Emploi) — [Observatoire des inégalités](https://www.inegalites.fr/Les-revenus-et-la-pauvrete-dans-les-quartiers-sensibles)
- Youth unemployment (under 30): 30.4% in QPV in 2020, about twice the rate in other neighbourhoods. Overall QPV unemployment fell from 26.7% (2014) to 18.6% (2020). Among people without a diploma, 22.2% are unemployed in QPV vs 15.3% elsewhere — [CIDJ citing ONPV](https://www.cidj.com/travailler/decrocher-un-emploi/quartiers-prioritaires-plus-de-chomage-notamment-chez-les-jeunes)
- Early school leavers: 17% of young people in QPV vs 8% in non-QPV urban areas (2021), down from 25% in 2007 — [Préfecture PACA webinar](https://www.prefectures-regions.gouv.fr/provence-alpes-cote-dazur/irecontenu/telechargement/124115/919039/file/Compte-rendu%20webinaire)
- Regional poverty figures: 37% in Île-de-France QPV ([INSEE Flash IdF](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/2019723)); 53% in Réunion QPV in 2019 ([INSEE](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/6800970)); 46% in Nouvelle-Aquitaine QPV in 2018 ([INSEE](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/7700492))
- 2015 QPV unemployment was 26.4% (ONPV 2016), but the measurement method has since changed, so it is not comparable — [Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/rapport-onpv-2016-le-chomage-baisse-un-tout-petit-peu-dans-les-quartiers)
- QPV geography: 1,514 QPV in 859 communes with 5.4 million inhabitants (1 Jan 2018). The new map (decree 2023-1314 of 28 Dec 2023, in force 1 Jan 2024) lists 1,362 QPV in metropolitan France (previously 1,296), adding about 200,000 residents. Criteria: at least 1,000 inhabitants, an urban unit of more than 10,000, and household income well below local and national levels — [Pyrénées-Atlantiques prefecture press release](https://www.pyrenees-atlantiques.gouv.fr/contenu/telechargement/50853/375298/file/240111_CP_Nouvelle%20g%C3%A9ographie%20prioritaire%20de%20la%20politique%20de%20la%20ville%20(QPV).pdf); [Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/nouvelle-geographie-prioritaire-les-elus-demandent-une-hausse-des-moyens-coherente-avec-la)
- In Bretagne, three-quarters of QPV housing is social housing, largely from the grands ensembles — [INSEE regional](https://www.insee.fr/fr/statistiques/fichier/8314262/br_inf_108.pdf)

### Inferences
- The QPV gap is persistent: unemployment fell in absolute terms between 2014 and 2022 but stayed at about 2.4 times the surrounding level. This supports structural ("relegation") explanations.

### Gaps
- I found no QPV-specific brevet or baccalauréat success rates for 2023-24.
- I did not obtain the share of immigrants or descendants of immigrants in QPV (INSEE puts it at roughly a quarter immigrants; not verified this session).

## Q3. Discrimination: ID checks, the Conseil d'État 2023 ruling, hiring tests

### Takeaway
Official and judicial bodies now acknowledge ethnic profiling. Young men perceived as Black or Arab are 4 times more likely to be checked and 12 times more likely to face an intrusive check (Défenseur des droits, 2024 survey). The 2017 survey gave a 20-fold risk. The Conseil d'État (11 Oct 2023) recognised that discriminatory checks are "not isolated" but refused to order reforms. In hiring, a candidate with a Maghrebi-sounding name is 31.5% less likely to get a callback (DARES 2021).

### Cited Findings
- Défenseur des droits, "Enquête Accès aux droits 2024" (published June 2025, more than 5,000 respondents): young men perceived as Black, Arab or Maghrebi are 4 times more exposed to a check and 12 times more likely to face an intrusive check (frisk, search). Identity checks number about 32 million a year (Cour des comptes 2023) — [DDD 2024 vol. 1](https://www.defenseurdesdroits.fr/sites/default/files/2025-06/ddd_EAD-2024_volume-1_relations-police-population.pdf); [press kit](https://www.defenseurdesdroits.fr/sites/default/files/2025-06/DDD-EAD-2024_volume1_DP.pdf)
- 2017 survey (fieldwork 2016): young men perceived as Black or Arab were 20 times more likely to be checked than the rest of the population. The methods differ from 2024, so the figures are not directly comparable — [DDD 2017](https://www.defenseurdesdroits.fr/sites/default/files/2023-10/ddd_etude_Enquete-acces-aux-droits-volume1_relations-police-population_20170111.pdf); [European Ombudsman network](https://eno.ombudsman.europa.eu/home/news/archives-2017/maincontent/relations-policepopulation--le-1.html)
- Conseil d'État, Assemblée, 11 Oct 2023, LDH et autres (n° 454836; class action by six NGOs including Amnesty and HRW): it recognised that discriminatory identity checks exist and are "not isolated", without calling them systemic. It refused to order measures, saying these fall to the legislator and executive and lie outside the court's powers — [Conseil d'État decision](https://www.conseil-etat.fr/fr/arianeweb/CE/decision/2023-10-11/454836); [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/fr/news/2023/10/19/le-conseil-detat-reconnait-les-controles-au-facies-mais-nordonne-pas-letat-dy); [Landot avocats](https://blog.landot-avocats.net/2023/10/11/les-controles-didentite-discriminatoires-ca-existe-et-ce-nest-pas-isole-sans-etre-systemique-constate-le-conseil-detat-mais-ce-nest-pas-une-raison-pour-demander-au-juge-administratif-ce-q/); [Amnesty France](https://www.amnesty.fr/discriminations/actualites/controles-au-facies-le-conseil-detat-reconnait-lexistence-du-probleme-mais-refuse-de-contraindre-letat-a-y-mettre-un-terme)
- Follow-up: in Nov 2024 the UN Human Rights Committee expressed concern about these checks — [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.fr/actualites/controles-au-facies-le-conseil-detat-reconnait-lexistence-du-probleme-mais-refuse-de-contraindre-letat-a-y-mettre-un-terme/). The ECtHR ruled in Seydi and others v. France on 26 June 2025 — [AN written question 10613](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/questions/QANR5L17QE10613)
- DARES/ISM Corum testing (DARES Analyses n°67, Nov 2021; 2,400 job offers, Dec 2019 to Apr 2021): callback rate 33.3% for French-sounding names vs 22.8% for Maghrebi-sounding names, a 31.5% lower chance. A Maghrebi-named candidate must send 1.5 times as many CVs to get the same number of callbacks. Discrimination is about twice as strong in low-skilled jobs — [DARES Analyses PDF](https://migrant-integration.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2022-01/Dares%20Analyses_testing_discrimination_embauche.pdf); [Observatoire des inégalités](https://inegalites.fr/Les-personnes-d-origine-maghrebine-nettement-discriminees-a-l-embauche); [IPP note 76](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-03471261v1/file/Note_IPP_76_Discrimination-a-l_embauche-selon-l_origine.pdf)

### Inferences
- Frequent, often unexplained checks are the everyday interface between banlieue youth and the state. They make police-related deaths an especially potent trigger, consistent with Roché's and Fassin's arguments (see Q7).

### Gaps
- I did not verify the IGPN annual report figures on complaints about discrimination.

## Q4. Police use of force: deaths, refusal-to-comply shootings, article L435-1

### Takeaway
The law of 28 Feb 2017 (article L435-1 of the Code de la sécurité intérieure) loosened the conditions under which police may fire at moving vehicles. According to a ministerial answer, 25 people died after refusing to comply with police between March 2017 and October 2022, vs 4 between July 2011 and February 2017. Sebastian Roché finds a fivefold increase in fatal shootings at vehicles. The government disputes the causal link. Nahel's death in 2023 fits this pattern.

### Cited Findings
- Article L435-1 CSI was created by the law of 28 Feb 2017 on public security. It allows use of weapons "in cases of absolute necessity and in a strictly proportionate manner" against drivers who are "likely" to endanger lives. LFI bills call for its repeal — [AN bill l16b0660](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/16/textes/l16b0660_proposition-loi.pdf); [AN bill l17b1212](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/textes/l17b1212_proposition-loi.pdf)
- Ministerial answer to a parliamentary question: 25 deaths linked to refusals to comply between March 2017 and October 2022, vs 4 between July 2011 and February 2017 — [AN written question 11446](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/16/questions/QANR5L16QE11446.pdf)
- Sebastian Roché (CNRS, Sciences Po Grenoble), in Esprit (Sep 2022): fatal police shootings at moving vehicles rose fivefold between 2012-2016 and 2017-2021. He attributes this to the law's broader latitude without matching training. He contrasts Germany, with one such fatal shooting in ten years — [Le Temps](https://www.letemps.ch/monde/vague-d-emotion-et-revolte-suite-a-la-mort-d-un-adolescent-tue-par-la-police-en-banlieue-parisienne)
- 13 people were killed after refusals to comply in 2022 vs 4 in 2021 (LFI MPs' figures) — [AN bill l16b1553](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/16/textes/l16b1553_proposition-loi.pdf)
- Government counter-position: the Interior Minister said police used firearms in 0.76% of refusal-to-comply cases in 2021 and claimed there were fewer shootings than before 2017. Opponents call this false — [AN QE 549](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/questions/QANR5L17QE549.pdf)
- Syndicat des avocats de France (lawyers' union) press release on refusal-to-comply shootings (2022) — [SAF](https://lesaf.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/CP-refus-dobtemp%C3%A9rer.pdf)
- In 2023 there were 10 judicial investigations into police conduct during the riots — [DH.be](https://www.dhnet.be/actu/monde/2023/07/05/emeutes-en-france-10-enquetes-sur-les-agissements-des-forces-de-lordre-XP55ZQAD6JA33MRKNW4EBGVH2I/)

### Inferences
- The causal effect of the 2017 law is contested. The rise is clear in raw counts, but the number of refusals to comply also grew, and the ministry and its critics use different scopes.

### Gaps
- IGPN annual totals of deaths during police operations were not retrieved.
- The legal outcome of the officer's case in the Nahel killing (referral for trial for murder, 2025) was not verified this session.

## Q5. Gilets jaunes (2018-19)

### Takeaway
The movement began on 17 Nov 2018 against a fuel-tax rise. Its base was working-class and lower-middle-class households in small towns and peri-urban areas, the "France périphérique" described by Guilluy. About 20% of French people identified as gilets jaunes. Repression was heavy: officially at least 2,000 protesters were injured, independent counts list 14 to 17+ people who lost an eye, and the IGPN opened 212 investigations. The government answered with the Grand débat (1.93 million online contributions, 10,452 local meetings).

### Cited Findings
- The trigger was opposition to a rise in fuel taxes — [La Vie des idées, "Fieldwork in a roundabout"](https://laviedesidees.fr/Fieldwork-in-a-roundabout)
- Composition: Pierre Blavier's roundabout ethnography (17 Nov 2018 to 15 Jan 2019) describes hundreds of thousands of mostly working-class people under tight household budget constraints — [La Vie des idées, "Le tour des ronds-points"](https://laviedesidees.fr/Le-tour-des-ronds-points); Bordeaux collective survey by Magali Della Sudda — [HAL](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-03098403v1/file/MDS%20HAL%20Un%20avant%20et%20un%20apre%CC%80s%20gilets%20jaunes%20reluMDS.pdf)
- 21% of 10,010 respondents identified as gilets jaunes (Institut Montaigne Baromètre des Territoires), consistent with the roughly 20% in Elabe/BFMTV polls — [Institut Montaigne](https://www.institutmontaigne.org/expressions/les-gilets-jaunes-la-partie-emergee-de-la-crise-sociale-francaise). Public support stood at 71% in one early poll — [Sud Radio](https://www.sudradio.fr/politique/sondage-71-des-francais-soutiennent-la-mobilisation-des-gilets-jaunes)
- Injuries: one MP's question to the government (2019) cites more than 2,000 protesters injured, more than 1,050 police and gendarmes injured, and 12 deaths overall (mostly road accidents at blockades) — [AN QG 1544](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/q15/15-1544QG.htm). Official Interior Ministry figures as of 9 May 2019: 1,630 security-force injuries — [Visionscarto poster](https://www.visionscarto.net/IMG/pdf/poster_beauvau.pdf)
- LBD (rubber-bullet launcher) injuries: Libération's tally lists at least 69 gilets jaunes or journalists injured by LBD and at least 14 who lost an eye. The collective "Désarmons-les" counts 17 eyes lost to LBD 40 and 4 hands torn off. Police sources acknowledged 4 eyes and 2 hands. Libération's Checknews counted 82 serious injuries — [Le Quotidien du Médecin](https://www.lequotidiendumedecin.fr/sante-societe/politique-de-sante/lanceurs-de-balles-de-defense-urgentistes-et-chirurgiens-decrivent-les-blessures); [Europe 1](https://www.europe1.fr/node/203567); [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/fil-info/belgiqueetmonde/2019/01/28/un-des-leaders-des-gilets-jaunes-blesse-cest-un-abattage-dans-les-regles-de-lart-JGJ4MEJOQBAURE6M7Z3LZPXEA4/) (counts vary with date and source)
- IGPN: 212 investigations opened into suspected police violence, 54 dismissed, and at the time of the answer one conviction (of a CRS officer) — [AN QOSD 923](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QOSD923.pdf)
- Journalists: at least 54 injured, 12 seriously, within six months (RSF) — [RSF](https://rsf.org/pt-br/node/35518)
- Grand débat national (Jan to Mar 2019): about 1.93 million contributions from 475,439 contributors; 10,452 local meetings (60% organised by elected officials); about 16,000 cahiers citoyens; cost EUR 12 million — [AN QG 1771 (Lecornu)](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/15/questions/QANR5L15QG1771.pdf); [Anacej](https://www.anacej.fr/_files/ugd/965dc9_e37d6d1f11cb473eb3403324c35a18d0.pdf); [Public Sénat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/politique/le-grand-debat-en-chiffres-139919). Mid-term breakdown of online themes: taxation and public spending 34%, ecological transition 25%, organisation of the state 21%, democracy 19% — [Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/grand-debat-mi-parcours-le-gouvernement-precise-les-modalites-de-traitement-des-donnees)
- Guilluy's "La France périphérique" (2014) contrasts globalised metropolises (Paris, Lyon, Bordeaux) with a majority working-class France in small and medium towns and rural areas. It was widely seen as foreshadowing the gilets jaunes. Academic geographers criticise it for selective data and oversimplification — [Misterprepa](https://misterprepa.net/france-peripherique/); [Bookmarks/Spectator](https://bookmarks.reviews/reviews/twilight-of-the-elites-prosperity-the-periphery-and-the-future-of-france); [Slate](https://www.slate.fr/story/54109/fn-stupide-gauche-guilluy)

### Inferences
- The gilets jaunes largely bypassed both unions and the banlieues. They represent a third strand: the peri-urban, car-dependent working class, distinct from strand A's union-led mobilisations and strand B's banlieue riots.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the Interior Ministry count of 282,000 participants on 17 Nov 2018 (widely cited).
- I did not verify the cost of the December 2018 and April 2019 measures (about EUR 10 billion and 17 billion are often cited).
- No exhaustive official tally of LBD injuries exists.

## Q6. 2023 pension reform

### Takeaway
Raising the legal retirement age from 62 to 64 drew opposition from 68-72% of the population (about 75-78% of working people) in Elabe polls. Turnout at the largest union days reached about 1.28 million (Interior Ministry, 7 March). The government used Article 49.3 on 16 Mar 2023 to pass it without a vote. The Conseil constitutionnel approved it in part on 14 Apr 2023.

### Cited Findings
- 19 Jan 2023: 1.12 million protesters according to the Interior Ministry, more than 2 million according to the CGT — [Public Sénat timeline](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/politique/reforme-des-retraites-les-dates-cles-de-trois-mois-de-crise-sociale-et-politique)
- 7 Mar 2023: 1.28 million according to the Interior Ministry (record). The CGT figure of 3.5 million is attributed to 7 March and 23 March; the source is unclear — [Vikidia](https://fr.vikidia.org/wiki/Mouvement_social_contre_la_r%C3%A9forme_des_retraites_en_France_de_2023) (weak source; verify with the CGT and the ministry)
- 16 Mar 2023: Élisabeth Borne used Article 49.3 to adopt the text without a vote of the deputies. Mobilisation intensified afterwards — [Vikidia](https://fr.vikidia.org/wiki/Mouvement_social_contre_la_r%C3%A9forme_des_retraites_en_France_de_2023); [Public Sénat](https://www.publicsenat.fr/actualites/politique/reforme-des-retraites-les-dates-cles-de-trois-mois-de-crise-sociale-et-politique)
- 14 Apr 2023: the Conseil constitutionnel found the reform partially compliant. The CGT had contested the use of an amending social-security financing law (LFRSS) combined with 49.3 — [CGT document](https://snjcgt.fr/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2023/04/CGT-Conseil-Constitutionnel-14-avril-2023.pdf); [FNEE-CGT note](https://equipementcgt.fr/IMG/pdf/fnee-cgt_note_cgt_suite_decisions_cc_20230420.pdf)
- Opinion: Elabe on 25 Jan 2023 found 72% opposed (40% very opposed) and 78% of working people opposed — [Elabe](https://elabe.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/20230125_elabe_bfmtv_les-francais-lexecutif-et-la-reforme-des-retraites.pdf). Elabe on 15 Mar 2023 found 68% opposed and 75% of working people — [Elabe](https://elabe.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/20230315_elabe_bfmtv_les-francais-et-la-reforme-des-retraites-vague-9.pdf)

### Inferences
- 2023 illustrates a "legitimacy gap". A legally valid procedure (49.3 plus partial validation by the Conseil constitutionnel) was used against a stable two-thirds majority opposed to the reform and a united union front (intersyndicale). This feeds the narrative that institutions bypass the street, a recurring theme since 1995.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact age change (62 to 64) or the vote on the censure motion (failed by 9 votes) against a fetched source; both are widely reported.
- I did not verify the Conseil constitutionnel's rejection of the RIP (shared-initiative referendum) bid.

## Q7. Longer history: the revolutionary tradition, 1968/1995/2006, the colonial and immigration context, and early banlieue riots

### Takeaway
Banlieue riots date back at least to 1971-1981 in the Lyon suburbs (Minguettes, Vaulx-en-Velin). The 1983 Marche pour l'égalité was their peaceful political counterpart. Colonial violence, notably the 17 Oct 1961 massacre of Algerian demonstrators in Paris (officially 3 dead; historians estimate dozens to 200 or more), forms part of the memory background.

### Cited Findings
- Summer 1981: the first large riots (the "hot summer"), in Minguettes/Vénissieux, Villeurbanne and Vaulx-en-Velin (Rhône), with rodeos and burned cars. Riots had occurred earlier (1971; Vaulx-en-Velin's La Grappinière in Sept 1979) — [Slate](https://www.slate.fr/france/76028/emeutes-mythe-ete-chaud); [Millénaire3 Grand Lyon](https://millenaire3.grandlyon.com/content/download/1287/17459); [Abdellali Hajjat, Cultures & Conflits](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-01059472v1/file/hajjat_CC_093_0011.pdf)
- Vaulx-en-Velin, Oct 1990: Thomas Claudio, 21, died on 6 Oct 1990 riding pillion on a motorbike during a police incident. Three days of "urban guerrilla" scenes were televised for the first time, embarrassing a ZUP (priority urbanisation zone) presented as a model of urban policy — [Lyon Capitale](https://www.lyoncapitale.fr/?p=68836)
- Marche pour l'égalité et contre le racisme, 1983: about 4,000 marchers gathered at Minguettes on 29 Oct and about 100,000 people greeted them at the Bastille on 3 Dec 1983 — [Lyon Capitale](https://www.lyoncapitale.fr/actualite/lyon-capitale-des-banlieues-citoyennes)
- 17 Oct 1961: the official toll was 3 dead; historians estimate "several dozen, possibly 200" dead and about 11,000 arrested (one source says "blessés" (injured), which is probably a mistake); some sources say 300 or more. An Élysée note of 28 Oct 1961 already suggests a high toll. Jean-Luc Einaudi's list of victims is contested by AASSDN, a partisan defence-establishment association. President Macron acknowledged the events in 2021 — [Le360](https://afrique.le360.ma/algerie-autres-pays/politique/2021/10/17/35747-massacre-du-17-octobre-1961-des-policiers-et-gendarmes-dune-brutalite-atroce-35747/); [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/international/europe/2021/10/16/17-octobre-1961-macron-sapprete-a-reconnaitre-une-verite-incontestable-C3LPCHT3PNHCLLP6EFJXAWCVTM/); [AN resolution l17b1899](https://questions.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/textes/l17b1899_proposition-resolution.pdf); [AASSDN (contrarian)](https://aassdn.org/amicale/17-octobre-1961-a-paris-un-massacre-reel-ou-invente/?print=pdf)
- May 1968: 13 May Paris march, 800,000 according to organisers vs 171,000 officially. Described as the largest social movement of the 20th century in France — [vl-media](https://vl-media.fr/les-5-manifestations-qui-ont-marquees-lhistoire/); [75secondes](https://www.75secondes.fr/?p=83425)
- 1995 (plan Juppé, presented 15 Nov 1995, on pensions and social security): strikes at the SNCF, RATP, La Poste, France Télécom and in education. 60% of the public supported the strikers at the peak, with 500,000 to 1 million marchers on a single day (CGT figure) — [Le Temps](https://www.letemps.ch/opinions/defaite-grevistes-francais); [WSWS](https://www.wsws.org/francais/News/1995/dec1995/revolt.shtml); [INA](https://fresques.ina.fr/sudorama/fiche-media/00000000105/manifestation-a-marseille-contre-le-plan-juppe.html)
- CPE 2006: a contract for people under 26 that allowed dismissal without stated reason during a two-year period — [75secondes](https://www.75secondes.fr/?p=83425)

### Inferences
- The two strands meet in their repertoire (the street and confrontation with the police) but differ in organisation. Strand A is union- or citizen-led and makes explicit demands. Strand B consists of leaderless riots by young people triggered by police incidents, with no explicit demands.

### Gaps (background, unverified this session)
- 1789, 1830, 1848 and the 1871 Commune as foundations of the street tradition; 1968 strike numbers (usually 7-10 million strikers) and the Grenelle agreements; the 1995 retreat on the pensions part of the plan; CPE turnout (about 1 to 3 million on 28 Mar and 4 Apr 2006) and its withdrawal on 10 Apr 2006; the Algerian War (1954-62); postwar labour immigration (ONI, Maghreb recruitment, the 1974 halt and family reunification); bidonvilles (Nanterre) and the construction of grands ensembles (about 1955-1975, ending with the 1973 Guichard circular); 2007 Villiers-le-Bel (Moushin and Larami; police officers hit by gunfire); 2017 Théo Luhaka in Aulnay-sous-Bois; Adama Traoré in 2016. None of these was confirmed by a source fetched this session.

## Q8. Academic explanations: competing interpretations

### Takeaway
The dominant sociological reading (Mucchielli, Mauger, Roché, Fassin, Roy) treats the riots as protest against relegation, discrimination and police humiliation, either "proto-political" or political without words. Culturalist and identity-focused readings (Kepel's religious-generational framing, Lagrange's "Le déni des cultures") stress family structures and religious or identity dynamics and drew strong controversy. Geographic and fragmentation readings (Guilluy, Fourquet) explain the gilets jaunes and the broader fracturing of society.

### Cited Findings
- Mucchielli and Le Goaziou, *Quand les banlieues brûlent… Retour sur les émeutes de novembre 2005* (La Découverte, 2006; enlarged 2007). It is based on interviews with residents and rioters and avoids casting young people as villains. Its framing: riots by mostly unemployed young people of immigrant origin have been recurrent for about 15 years — [KrimDok](https://krimdok.uni-tuebingen.de/Record/510535313); [French-American Foundation report](https://frenchamerican.org/wp-content/uploads/the-french-riots-of-2005-lessons-and-policy-responses.pdf)
- Lagrange and Oberti (eds.), *Émeutes urbaines et protestations* (Presses de Sciences Po, 2006). Valérie Sala Pala's review essay situates it among the 2005 literature — [HAL review](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-00476633v1/file/recension_FPCS_finale_2_pdf.pdf)
- Hugues Lagrange, *Le déni des cultures* (2010): drawing on more than 4,000 pupils (1999-2006) in the Paris suburbs matched with police records, he found young people of Sahelian origin over-represented in delinquency. He attributes this partly to cultural factors (paternal authoritarianism, polygamy, women's lack of autonomy). He was accused of "culturalism"; demographer Solène Lardoux and Michel Kokoreff wrote critiques, while Michèle Tribalat reviewed it more favourably — [La Vie des idées (Kokoreff)](https://laviedesidees.fr/Quartiers-et-differences.html); [La Vie des idées (Lardoux)](https://laviedesidees.fr/Objections-d-une-demographe); [Futuribles](https://futuribles.com/en/?p=40903); [Connection Ivoirienne/Regards](https://connectionivoirienne.net/2010/11/08/l%e2%80%99origine-culturelle-peut-elle-expliquer-la-delinquance/)
- Olivier Roy (2005): not a Muslim revolt but a spontaneous, generational, social movement of young people in deprived neighbourhoods, with no religious slogans and a Western urban street-wear culture — [Roy text, diplomatie.gouv.fr](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/IMG/pdf/0501-ROY-FR-2.pdf)
- Gilles Kepel: calls 2005 a "pivot year" for Islam in France, citing the two triggers (the electrocutions and a tear-gas grenade near a Clichy mosque) and a "third generation" of French Muslims raised on cyberculture. In the Kepel-Roy debate, Kepel sees "the radicalisation of Islam" and Roy "the Islamisation of radicalism" — [Slate](https://www.slate.fr/story/112465/djihad-francais); [Policy Exchange](https://policyexchange.org.uk/?p=27328); [Livres Hebdo](https://www.livreshebdo.fr/article/kepel-contre-roy)
- Didier Fassin, *La Force de l'ordre* (Seuil, 2011): an ethnography of BAC (anti-crime brigade) units in a Paris-area banlieue. It documents humiliation and discriminatory practice in routine policing as background to the riots — [Warwick reading list](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/arts/modernlanguages/applying/undergraduate/frenchmodules/fr325ps/outline/policing14)
- Sebastian Roché: links police doctrine and the 2017 law to the fatal shootings (see Q4) — [Le Temps](https://www.letemps.ch/monde/vague-d-emotion-et-revolte-suite-a-la-mort-d-un-adolescent-tue-par-la-police-en-banlieue-parisienne)
- Jérôme Fourquet, *L'Archipel français* (2019): France fragmented into separate "islands", a gap between the country as it is and its representations of itself — [Le Temps](https://www.letemps.ch/monde/2022-une-france-ordinaire-guerre-contre-inegalites)
- Guilluy: see Q5. He is criticised for selective data and for echoing immigration concerns drawn from Tribalat — [Bookmarks](https://bookmarks.reviews/reviews/twilight-of-the-elites-prosperity-the-periphery-and-the-future-of-france)
- Security-focused and political reading (2023): figures such as "they no longer respect France" and "Nahel's death is a pretext" frame the riots as delinquency or rejection of the nation rather than protest. The Interior Ministry stressed that many of those arrested were unknown to police — [Sud Radio](https://www.sudradio.fr/parlons-vrai-chez-bourdin/emeutes-la-mort-de-nahel-est-un-pretexte-ils-ne-respectent-plus-la-france); [thesun.my/AFP](https://thesun.my/?p=340257)

### Inferences
- The "unknown to police" finding cuts both ways. It weakens the claim that the riots were driven by gangs or drug networks, but it also shows that participation reached beyond hardened delinquents, which supports grievance-based readings.
- The two readings are not mutually exclusive. The sociological literature mostly concedes that looting and consumerist motives existed in 2023 alongside anti-police anger.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a primary source for Gérard Mauger's "proto-political revolt" thesis (*L'émeute de novembre 2005: une révolte protopolitique*, 2006) in this session.
- I found no primary source for Roché's *La nation inachevée* (2022) on young people's attachment to the nation, or for detailed Fourquet data.
