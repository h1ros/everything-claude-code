## 1. What unrest events have happened in France in 2025–2026 (to Oct 2026), and what triggered each?

### Takeaway
France is in a real, verifiable wave of unrest as of mid-October 2026. A school-student (lycéen) blockade movement started in September 2026. Union strike and march days followed (6, 8 and 13 Oct). Then on Saturday 17 October 2026 came a nationwide day of action against the cost of living. It began as a TikTok call and quickly drew in gilets-jaunes networks. About 550,000 people marched by official count, and the day brought serious rioting and looting in Paris, Marseille, Bordeaux, Lyon and Limoges. Before this came the 2025 wave ("Bloquons tout" on 10 Sept 2025 and the union days of 18 Sept and 2 Oct 2025). There were also separate non-political riots after the PSG football wins (May 2025 and May 2026), and a far-left/far-right street-violence episode in Lyon (Feb 2026).

**2025 run-up**

By late afternoon the Interior Ministry counted 175,000 participants across 550 gatherings and 262 blockades, with 473 arrests (203 in Paris) and 339 people in police custody (106 in Paris). Bruno Retailleau (then Interior Minister) said in the evening there were "fewer than 200,000" participants — [Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/bloquons-tout-premier-etat-des-lieux); [Brut](https://www.brut.media/fr/articles/bloquons-tout-des-dizaines-de-milliers-de-manifestants-des-tensions-mais-pas-de-paralysie)

### Inferences
- The October 2026 events combine three forms of unrest:
  - a peaceful mass protest led by youth and unions;
  - a leaderless, social-media-born "colère" movement in the gilets-jaunes mould, centred on fuel and cost of living and calling for Macron's resignation;
  - significant rioting and looting on its margins, especially on 17 Oct.
- None of the 2026 episodes found is a classic banlieue riot triggered by a police killing, like Nahel in 2023. Searches for one returned nothing. The closest analogues are the PSG riots (sport-related) and the Lyon political street violence.
- The 17 Oct "V2" call echoes 2018 (fuel-tax trigger, social-media origin, Élysée as target). The 10 Sept 2025 "Bloquons tout" is the immediate organisational precedent.

## 2. Official vs organiser figures: turnout, arrests, injuries, damage (with discrepancies)

### Takeaway
For 17 Oct 2026 the official figure is about 550,000 nationwide, against about 800,000 from unions and organisers. Arrest totals climbed during the weekend: about 400 on the evening of the 17th, then 1,200, then 1,800, then 2,397 (consolidated). Injured police and gendarmes went from about 150 to 997, 7 of them seriously. One protester died (a medical collapse in Paris), and a 17-year-old lost a hand to a grenade in Saint-Étienne. Turnout figures differ by 2–14x in some cities (Bordeaux 7,000 vs 100,000).

## 3. Stated demands and organisers

### Takeaway
The lycéens demand material school improvements: more teachers, smaller classes, decent buildings, changes to Parcoursup, and some oppose military spending priorities. The 17 Oct "colère" movement demands lower fuel prices and action on the cost of living, more money for public services, an end to police violence, and the resignation of Macron and the government. It has no formal leader: a TikTok creator ("V2") made the original call, gilets-jaunes networks relayed it, and unions joined. The CGT, Solidaires and Confédération paysanne backed convergence on 17 Oct without leading it. The full intersyndicale, including the CFDT, backed the lycéen days (6 Oct).

### Cited Findings
- Lycéen demands: more teachers and staff, smaller classes, decent buildings — [Café pédagogique](https://www.cafepedagogique.net/2026/10/01/lyceens-mobilises-ce-quils-disent-ce-quon-leur-repond/); [CNews explainer](https://www.cnews.fr/france/2026-09-29/blocus-des-lycees-que-revendiquent-vraiment-les-eleves-1926271)

  - CNN describes the day as largely youth- and union-led — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/17/europe/half-million-protesters-sweep-france-in-youth-unrest-latam-intl)

### Inferences
- The organising pattern is hybrid: a leaderless online call, then union support. This makes negotiation hard, because no one can "call off" the movement. That fits the gilets-jaunes precedent.
- The demands combine youth and education grievances with an anti-austerity, cost-of-living frame. Both connect to the 2027 austerity budget (see Q5).

## 4. Government response (policing, legislation, concessions)

### Takeaway
The response combines heavy policing (mass arrests, LBD and grenade use now under IGPN scrutiny) with firm rhetoric. On 18 Oct Macron also opened a political door: he split "legitimate" demonstrators from casseurs, created a crisis cell under PM Lecornu to help victims and shopkeepers, let prefects ban specific gatherings, and announced a "national consultation". For the lycéens, the Education Minister offered a consultation platform. No legislative or budget concession had been found as of 19 Oct.

### Cited Findings
- Macron on 17 Oct: people who looted and damaged property, including town halls, will be "prosecuted and punished" — [CNews](https://www.cnews.fr/france/2026-10-17/mobilisation-du-17-octobre-les-casseurs-seront-poursuivis-et-punis-promet). He vowed to hold accountable those who "sought to bring down the Republic" — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/17/europe/half-million-protesters-sweep-france-in-youth-unrest-latam-intl)
- Macron's 18 Oct address — [Boursorama/Reuters](https://www.boursorama.com/actualite-economique/actualites/france-macron-dit-vouloir-punir-les-casseurs-et-ecouter-les-manifestants-89a6f45e5378cd2cfc32abd5258530f1); [Entrevue](https://entrevue.fr/entrevue/violences-du-17-octobre-macron-promet-detre-intraitable-avec-les-casseurs/); [Atlasinfo](https://atlasinfo.fr/le-president-macron-promet-la-transparence-et-la-fermete.html):

## 5. Link to the fiscal situation and political deadlock since the 2024 dissolution

### Takeaway
Since the June 2024 dissolution there has been no majority in the Assembly. Two PMs (Barnier, Bayrou) fell over austerity plans. Sébastien Lecornu was appointed in Sept 2025, resigned, and was reappointed on 10 Oct 2025. He passed the 2026 budget only through three uses of 49.3, finally adopted on 2 Feb 2026 after censure motions failed. On 1 Oct 2026 he presented a "belt-tightening" 2027 budget, France's last before the April 2027 presidential election: about €54bn of savings and taxes, with public-sector wages and most pensions frozen. It came out in the same days as the lycée blockades and just before 17 Oct. The government's own budget papers project debt at 121.7% of GDP. Meanwhile the RN (Bardella, and Le Pen, eligible again after her July 2026 appeal) leads polls for 2027.
